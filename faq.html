Your first sound bath

What happens, what to wear, and how to prepare.

What exactly happens during a sound bath?

A sound bath is an opportunity to step away from the demands of everyday life and simply listen. There’s nothing you need to accomplish, nothing you need to fix, and no particular way you’re supposed to feel.

During my sessions, participants rest on the floor or in suspended hammocks, depending on the event. I use large-format crystal singing bowls, planetary gongs, Tibetan bowls, chimes, and other instruments to create an immersive listening experience.

I encourage people to notice the sound, the silence between the sounds, and what they experience within their own bodies. Every session is different, and every person’s experience is their own.

How do I prepare for my first sound bath experience?

The most important thing you can bring is an open mind.

Arrive early enough to get comfortable. Wear comfortable clothing, bring a blanket if appropriate, silence your phone, and allow yourself to experience something unfamiliar without expectations.

You don’t need previous meditation experience. You don’t need to understand frequencies or own a singing bowl.

You don’t even need to know how to relax.

Just come as you are.

What are seven things to know before your first sound bath?
  1. You don’t need previous meditation experience.
  2. You don’t need to clear your mind.
  3. Wear comfortable clothing and arrive early.
  4. You are allowed to move and adjust your position.
  5. Some instruments may produce powerful sounds and physical sensations.
  6. Your experience may be completely different from the person next to you.
  7. There is nothing you need to accomplish.

The last one is probably the most important.

We spend so much of our lives trying to become something, improve something, or fix something.

For this period of time, you can simply be.

What should I wear to a sound bath meditation?

Wear whatever allows you to be comfortable for an extended period of time. Loose-fitting clothing, comfortable pants, socks, and layers are all good choices.

Your body may become cooler as you settle into rest, so bringing a blanket is always a good idea.

This isn’t a fashion show. Comfort is more important than appearance.

What should I eat before or after a sound bath?

I recommend arriving comfortably fed, but not immediately after eating a large meal.

Drink water, avoid excessive alcohol, and give yourself enough time to arrive without rushing.

Afterward, have water or something nourishing if you feel hungry.

There is no special diet, cleansing process, or detoxification protocol required to participate in a sound bath.

How long should a sound bath session last?

Most of my sound experiences include approximately 45 to 60 minutes of sound, with additional time before and afterward.

I believe the time before the first instrument is played and the time after the last sound fades are important parts of the experience.

I don’t want participants rushing into the room, immediately lying down, and then jumping back into their daily lives the moment the sound ends.

The transition into and out of the experience matters.

What’s the best time of day for a sound bath?

There is no wrong time of day to create space for yourself.

A morning sound bath can help you begin your day with intention. An afternoon session can provide a break from your responsibilities, while an evening session can help you transition toward rest.

Many of my public sound baths are scheduled in the evening because it gives people an opportunity to leave the demands of their day behind.

What if I fall asleep during a sound bath?

It’s perfectly fine to fall asleep.

People often apologize afterward because they think they’ve missed something or haven’t participated correctly.

You don’t owe me your attention for the entire session.

If your body needs rest, allow yourself to rest. If you remain awake and aware of every sound, that’s equally valid.

If snoring becomes disruptive to others, we may gently encourage a change in position. Otherwise, there is no need to worry about falling asleep.

Should I do anything special after a sound bath?

Give yourself a little time before returning to your normal routine.

One of the things I value in my sessions is the silence after the last instrument has been played.

I don’t want to immediately fill that space with conversation, instructions, or unnecessary noise.

Allow yourself to transition naturally. Drink some water, stretch gently if you want to, and notice how you feel.

You don’t need to analyze the experience, document it, or explain it to someone else.

Sometimes an experience can simply remain an experience.

What are the most common questions people ask about sound baths?

People often ask whether they need meditation experience, whether they will fall asleep, whether they can move, and whether they will physically feel the instruments.

You don’t need experience. You can fall asleep. You can adjust your position. And depending on the instruments and where you’re positioned, you may physically feel some of the sound.

One of the biggest misconceptions is that people think they’re supposed to experience something extraordinary.

You are not being evaluated. There is no correct experience.

Comfort & safety

Sound sensitivity, pregnancy, children, and honoring your boundaries.

Is a sound bath safe? Who should avoid sound baths?

Sound baths are generally well tolerated, but sound affects everyone differently.

People with tinnitus, significant sound sensitivity, sound-triggered migraines, or certain neurological conditions may need additional considerations.

If you have a medical condition that concerns you, speak with your healthcare provider and communicate with your practitioner before attending.

I encourage participants to honor their boundaries. You are always welcome to reposition yourself, sit up, use hearing protection, or leave the room if necessary.

What happens if you’re sensitive to sound?

Being sensitive to sound doesn’t automatically mean you cannot attend a sound bath, but it’s important to communicate with the practitioner beforehand.

My larger gongs and crystal singing bowls can produce powerful sounds. I understand that what feels comfortable to one person may be overwhelming to someone else.

Depending on your sensitivity, we may choose a position farther away from the instruments, consider hearing protection, or discuss whether a gentler private session would be more appropriate.

You are never expected to tolerate discomfort to receive a benefit.

Is a sound bath safe during pregnancy?

Pregnancy is an individual experience, and I don’t make blanket statements about what is appropriate for every pregnant participant.

I recommend discussing participation with your prenatal healthcare provider, particularly when an experience involves large gongs, powerful singing bowls, or suspended hammocks.

Please let me know beforehand so we can discuss positioning, comfort, sound intensity, and any necessary accommodations.

Your comfort and safety come before the experience itself.

Can kids do sound baths? Is it safe for children?

Children can participate in age-appropriate sound experiences, but the environment needs to be adapted to their needs.

A child may not be comfortable lying still for an hour, and some instruments can be particularly intense for sensitive ears.

Shorter sessions, lower volumes, parental supervision, and the freedom to move are important considerations.

Not every adult sound bath is appropriate for children, so I recommend checking with the practitioner before bringing them.

The practice

How sound meditation works, the instruments, and getting the most from it.

Sound bath vs. meditation: What’s the difference?

I consider a sound bath a form of meditation. More specifically, I consider it a listening practice.

Traditional meditation may involve focusing on your breath, repeating a mantra, or observing your thoughts. During a sound meditation, the sound provides another point of focus.

You don’t have to clear your mind. You don’t have to stop thinking. You simply have an opportunity to return to listening whenever your attention wanders.

Why do sound baths feel different than other meditations?

Sound has both an auditory and a physical quality.

When I play a 38-inch gong or a 36-inch crystal singing bowl, you’re not necessarily just hearing the instrument. Depending on your position and the sound being produced, you may also feel it.

The combination of tones, overtones, resonance, and silence creates an experience that can be very different from a guided meditation.

You don’t need to follow a voice or visualize anything.

The sound becomes an invitation to listen.

What instruments are used in sound baths?

My collection includes instruments that create a wide range of tones, textures, and vibrations:

  • Large-format crystal singing bowls, including bowls measuring up to 36 inches.
  • Paiste planetary gongs, including Earth, Sun, Sedna, Saturn, and Chiron.
  • Tibetan singing bowls.
  • Ocean drums.
  • Koshi and other chimes.
  • Crystal triangles.
  • Rav Vast drums.
  • Monochord and other resonant instruments.

Each instrument has its own character. Knowing when to introduce an instrument, how to play it, and when to allow silence is just as important as the instrument itself.

What are the best sound frequencies for deep meditation and relaxation?

I don’t believe there is one magical frequency that works for everyone.

You’ll often hear people talk about 432 Hz, 528 Hz, planetary frequencies, and other specific tones. While these can be interesting to explore, I don’t make medical or healing claims based on a particular frequency.

My approach involves the relationship between tones, overtones, resonance, volume, rhythm, and silence.

A frequency is only one element of a much larger experience.

How often should you do sound baths for the best results?

There is no prescribed number of sound baths someone needs to attend.

Some people attend weekly, others monthly, and some come whenever they feel the need to reconnect with themselves.

I encourage people to develop a regular practice of listening and rest, whether that’s through attending a sound bath or simply creating moments of quiet throughout their day.

It isn’t about how many sound baths you attend. It’s about the relationship you develop with yourself.

How do I get the most benefit from a sound bath meditation?

Stop trying to get something out of it.

One of the challenges many of us face is that we’ve turned even relaxation into another task that needs to be accomplished.

We want to meditate correctly, relax faster, heal something, or leave feeling different.

Instead, I encourage you to listen without constantly evaluating your experience.

Allow the sound to come and go. Notice the silence. Notice yourself.

There is nothing to achieve.

How long does it take to feel the benefits of sound baths?

Some people notice a difference during their very first experience. Others develop a deeper appreciation for the practice over time.

Some people leave feeling relaxed, while others may feel energized, reflective, or simply unchanged.

I encourage people not to measure the success of a sound bath solely by how they feel immediately afterward.

The practice of regularly creating time for yourself can be valuable without every experience needing to be transformative.

Can I do a sound bath at home? What do I need?

You don’t need thousands of dollars’ worth of instruments to develop a listening practice at home.

You can begin with a singing bowl, a recording, gentle music, or even the natural sounds around you.

Find somewhere comfortable, eliminate distractions, put your phone away, and give yourself permission to do nothing for a while.

A recording is different from experiencing large instruments in person, particularly when it comes to the physical sensation of sound, but both can have a place in your practice.

Can you become addicted to sound baths? Is it possible to do too many?

Enjoying sound baths and wanting to attend regularly doesn’t mean you’re addicted to them.

However, I think it’s important to consider our relationship with any wellness practice.

If we believe that we cannot relax without a particular instrument, practitioner, or experience, we may be placing too much responsibility for our well-being outside ourselves.

My intention is not to make people dependent on attending my sound baths.

I would rather someone discover that listening, presence, and moments of rest are available to them in their everyday lives.

Sound meditation is a practice, not an escape from everything else.

Sound & wellbeing

Stress, sleep, emotions, and what the research says.

Will a sound bath help my anxiety and stress?

Many people attend my sound baths because they are overwhelmed, stressed, or simply tired of constantly being on the go.

Sound meditation may help create an environment where the body can settle and the mind can become less occupied with everyday demands.

I don’t promise to eliminate anxiety or treat a medical condition. What I offer is a space where, for a little while, nothing is being asked of you.

Sometimes that alone can be meaningful.

Can sound baths help with depression and low mood?

Sound meditation may offer an opportunity for rest, connection, and a temporary break from the demands of everyday life.

For someone experiencing depression, even participating in a quiet activity alongside other people may feel meaningful.

However, depression is a serious health condition, and sound baths are not a substitute for professional mental health care.

I welcome people as they are. You don’t have to arrive feeling positive, and you don’t have to pretend you’re feeling better when you leave.

Sound bath for sleep: Can it help insomnia?

Many people find sound meditation relaxing, and some fall asleep during my sessions.

Creating a transition between the demands of the day and a period of quiet may help support a better evening routine.

However, a sound bath is not a treatment for insomnia, and I wouldn’t recommend replacing medical care with sound meditation.

Think of it as another opportunity to practice rest rather than something that must put you to sleep.

Can sound baths help athletes with recovery?

Athletes spend a considerable amount of time challenging their bodies. Rest deserves just as much attention as physical performance.

A sound bath can provide dedicated time away from training, competition, screens, and other demands.

While I don’t claim that sound meditation repairs muscles or accelerates physical recovery, it can complement a broader wellness routine that includes appropriate nutrition, sleep, and physical recovery.

Not every activity needs to involve pushing yourself further.

Why do sound baths make people cry? Is that normal?

Sometimes when we stop moving, stop talking, and stop distracting ourselves, we become more aware of what we’ve been carrying.

I’ve seen people experience a wide range of emotions during sound meditations. Some cry, some smile, some sleep, and others simply enjoy the experience.

I don’t try to interpret someone’s emotions for them. Crying doesn’t necessarily mean that trauma is being released or that something profound has happened.

Whatever comes up belongs to you. There is no need to explain it or make it mean something.

Sound bath for healing: What does research say?

I intentionally describe myself as a sound bath practitioner rather than a sound healer.

There is emerging research exploring the relationship between sound meditation, stress, relaxation, and emotional well-being. However, much of that research is preliminary, and it doesn’t establish that sound baths cure diseases or heal specific medical conditions.

I’ve had sound as part of my life and practice for 19 years. I’ve witnessed meaningful experiences, but personal experiences are not the same as scientific evidence.

I don’t need to make extraordinary claims about sound to recognize the value of creating space for people to rest.

Sound bath vs. sound healing: What’s the real difference?

This distinction is important to me.

I call myself a sound bath practitioner, not a sound healer. I don’t believe it’s my place to tell someone what needs healing or to suggest that I possess the ability to heal them.

My role is to facilitate an experience through sound, silence, and intentional listening.

What happens within that experience belongs to the individual.

I can create the environment. I can play the instruments. I can hold space. But I don’t need to take ownership of someone else’s personal experience.

Private sessions, events & pricing

Booking Wyatt for yourself, a couple, or a gathering.

How much does a private sound bath session cost?

The cost depends on the number of participants, location, duration, and the instruments involved.

Contact me for your personal quote.

A private sound bath involves considerably more than the time spent playing instruments. Transportation, equipment, setup, preparation, and creating an appropriate environment are all part of the experience.

Why are sound baths so expensive? Are they worth the cost?

What you experience during a one-hour sound bath is only a portion of the work involved.

My collection includes large-format crystal singing bowls, some measuring 36 inches, and planetary gongs measuring up to 38 inches. Transporting, maintaining, setting up, and safely working with these instruments requires time, experience, and a significant investment.

There are also years of education and practice behind what I do.

At the same time, I believe sound meditation should be accessible. That’s why I offer different experiences at different price points and work with community organizations.

Whether it’s worth the cost is something each person gets to decide for themselves.

Can I hire a sound bath practitioner for my event or party?

Yes. I offer private sound bath experiences for birthdays, retreats, corporate gatherings, wellness events, and other special occasions.

However, I approach these experiences differently than someone hired to provide background music or entertainment. A sound bath is an intentional listening experience.

I work with the host to create an environment where participants can disconnect from distractions and become fully present.

How do I plan a sound bath event?

Start by considering the purpose of the gathering, the number of participants, and the environment.

When I plan a private sound bath, I consider the acoustics of the room, outside noise, available floor space, accessibility, lighting, and the instruments appropriate for that particular setting.

My events typically require considerable setup and breakdown time, especially when I’m bringing large-format crystal bowls, planetary gongs, or suspended hammocks.

The environment is part of the experience. It deserves just as much attention as the sound itself.

Can couples do a sound bath together?

Yes, and what I appreciate about couples sound baths is that they allow two people to share an experience without needing to talk or do anything for one another.

So much of our time together revolves around responsibilities, decisions, and communication.

A sound bath offers something different: the opportunity to simply exist together.

I offer private couples sound immersions for those who want a more personal and intimate listening experience.

Group sound baths vs. private sessions: Which should I choose?

Group sound baths offer the opportunity to share an experience with others without necessarily having to interact with them.

There is something meaningful about a room full of people who have collectively decided to stop, rest, and listen.

Private sessions allow me to create a more individualized experience, including instrument selection, positioning, and the overall intensity of the sound.

Neither is necessarily better. They serve different purposes.

How do I find a professional sound bath practitioner near me?

Look beyond the photographs and the instruments. Find someone who understands how to work with sound, volume, silence, and the people in the room.

Ask about their training, experience, and approach to facilitating a session. Owning singing bowls doesn’t necessarily make someone an experienced sound bath practitioner.

I facilitate sound meditations throughout Reno–Sparks, Nevada, working with yoga studios, wellness centers, community organizations, and private clients.

How do I choose a sound bath practitioner?

Ask about their experience, education, instruments, and philosophy.

My own introduction to sound began here in Reno and eventually led me to study with gong master Judy Strauss. What started as curiosity became something that has remained part of my life for 19 years.

A practitioner should understand that they’re working with people, not simply instruments.

Pay attention to whether they respect personal boundaries, manage sound levels thoughtfully, and create an environment where participants feel comfortable making their own choices.

What questions should I ask a sound bath practitioner?

I would encourage you to ask questions such as:

  • How long have you been working with sound?
  • Who have you studied with?
  • What instruments do you use?
  • How do you manage sound intensity?
  • What happens if I become uncomfortable?
  • Can you accommodate physical limitations or sound sensitivities?
  • What is your philosophy regarding sound meditation and healing?

A practitioner should be comfortable answering these questions.

You should never feel pressured to accept claims, beliefs, or practices that don’t resonate with you.
